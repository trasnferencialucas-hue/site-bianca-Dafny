Eloping in the Algarve: the complete guide

Home/Journal/Weddings

How to plan a ceremony for two in southern Portugal: legalities, locations, budget and the ideal rhythm of a day without guests.

An elopement isn't a small wedding. It's a different thing entirely: a day built around two people, with no guest timings, no top table and no speeches. In the Algarve, with our climate and coastline, it works particularly well.

Getting legally married in Portugal

Foreign nationals can legally marry in Portugal, but the civil registry process takes time and requires translated paperwork. The route most couples choose is to handle the legal part at home and hold a symbolic ceremony in Portugal, led by a celebrant — same emotion, zero bureaucracy.

The best places to elope

  • Sagres and the Costa Vicentina, for wind, cliffs and drama
  • Praia da Marinha and Benagil, for the classic golden rock
  • The Ria Formosa dunes, discreet and nearly always empty
  • Countryside estates between Loulé, Silves and Monchique
  • The Alvor boardwalks at sunrise

Some beaches require a municipal permit for ceremonies with any structure. For a simple elopement with two people and a celebrant it's rarely needed — but I always check first.

Without guests, the day stops having a schedule. It starts having light.

One possible day

A calm start in the early afternoon, getting ready at your accommodation. Ceremony about two hours before sunset, when the sun is low enough that nobody squints. Then an hour of walking, just for pictures, through the whole golden hour. A late dinner somewhere by the sea. None of it needs to race the clock.

A realistic budget

Counting photography, celebrant, flowers, a good dinner and two or three nights' accommodation, most Algarve elopements land between €2,500 and €6,000 — a fraction of a traditional wedding, with the whole day devoted to the two of you.
